# Efficiency review — M2-09: authorised hybrid retrieval pipeline

Every feature records this before it counts as done. The code is
`internal/rag/query`; the figures come from `go test -run TestBenchQueryPipeline` in that package
(env-gated, see its header). They are **dev box** figures (Windows 11, shared with other jobs and
antivirus), neither emulated nor certified, from a deterministic **fake** embedder and reranker: they
measure the pipeline and its overhead around the models, never a model.

## Requirement
Scoped retrieval, inside the Lite profile and the bounded-resource and graceful-degradation rules:

- **Pipeline.** deterministic filters and exact identifiers, then authorised FTS5/BM25, then authorised
  vectors, then Reciprocal Rank Fusion (k = 60, configurable), then optional parent expansion, then an
  optional reranker (Reranker role), then a final authorisation and version recheck, then a bounded
  `EvidencePackage`. Every item keeps its source's egress mode and classification (most restrictive when
  the declaration states none) so a later data-egress filter can act without querying again.
- **Authority.** Scope is applied in the stores, before ranking, and checked again at every hand-over. A
  source revoked or re-indexed while the query runs must be absent from the package. A failed read of
  authorisation state is an error, never an empty package.
- **Degradation (§18).** No embedder or vector index: FTS-only, labelled `reduced_semantic_recall`. No
  reranker: fused order. Rewrite is for low-confidence searches only, inside a fixed time and query
  budget. The reranker is off in Lite.
- **Footprint (§17.2).** Capped candidates, parent expansion, evidence tokens, terms and time; no
  always-on service, model, cache or background goroutine of its own.
- **Latency.** No promise from a headline number. ADR 0032 measured BM25 inside a large scope as slow
  (a 50,000-chunk scope: AND2 p95 250 to 325 ms, OR16 about 1 s, four readers), so the FTS stage is
  capped and budgeted and this review reports p50 and p95 per scope size.

## No-model / deterministic option
This is the first candidate and it is the fallback, so it is built, not skipped.

- **FTS-only is the no-model pipeline.** BM25 over the caller's own rowid range, exact identifiers pinned
  ahead of everything (part numbers, SKUs found by B-tree range scan, not by a model), parent expansion
  by a SQL read, a recheck by SQL. No embedder, no vector, no reranker, no router: zero model RAM, zero
  model calls. It runs whenever the Embedder role is absent or unhealthy, and says so in the package.
  What it cannot do is find a paraphrase or a question in another language; that is the one thing the
  vector stage is for.
- **The rewrite has a no-model form too.** `VariantRewriter` writes the question again with every plain
  word in its other inflection (the index does not stem, so "warranties" does not match "warranty"): one
  extra full-text pass, no model. The model form (`ModelRewriter`, Router role, schema-constrained, 128
  tokens at most) is opt-in and off. Both are off by default.
- **Considered as a no-model alternative to the vector stage: widening the full-text stage** (more terms).
  It is already any-term and it is the slow part of the pipeline: the term-cap table shows every extra
  term costing latency, and no word list finds a paraphrase.
- **How much recall the vector stage adds on real text is not measured here.** It needs the real
  embedder and a labelled corpus: M2-14 (harness) and M2-15 (live, Granite Embedding 97M via LocalAI).
  What this task proves is the mechanism, hermetically: a chunk with no word in common with the
  question is found by the vector stage alone, read back under the caller's scope, authorised and put in
  the package (`TestVectorOnlyCandidateIsHydrated`).

Not benchmarked, with the reason:

- **Real embedder and reranker latency and RSS** (Granite Embedding 97M, a compact reranker): they need
  LocalAI and the models, and this task is hermetic. M2-07 measured the vector index itself; M2-15 owes

  256 estimated tokens each, so about 3,000 tokens a call).
- **The model query rewrite (`ModelRewriter`)**: same reason, and it is off by default. Its cost is one
  Router call of at most 128 output tokens plus the extra full-text passes below.
- **The no-model rewrite's end-to-end cost.** It adds at most `max_rewrites` (2) further full-text passes

## Decision
**FTS-only is the floor; hybrid is the default whenever an embedder and a vector index are configured;
the reranker and the rewrite are off.** The smallest thing that passes comes first, and each step up is
justified by what it adds, not by what it costs:

- **Hybrid over FTS-only.** With the fix, the vector stage adds 4 to 19 ms and the read-back 1 to 2 ms to
  a 10k to 50k scope (the full-text stage is 43 to 235 ms and runs beside the embedding), and no new
  process or file. Its RAM is the vector cache dial of ADR 0011: 50 to 65 MiB over FTS-only at 50,000
  chunks with the 64 MiB cache, about 25 MiB with the cache off, where the scan costs 152 ms a query
  instead of 19.
- **Reranker off in Lite (§7.1).** Measured here it is 0.1 ms of overhead around a fake; the cost that
  matters is the model's, unmeasured, so it stays an opt-in with a bounded input and a fallback to the
  fused order.
- **Rewrite off.** It spends full-text passes, the scarce resource, on a minority of questions. The
  no-model `VariantRewriter` is the one to enable first; the model rewriter is the larger candidate and
  needs a measured gain to justify a Router call.
- **Defaults** (`rag_query` section, all validated, none clamped): RRF k 60; `max_query_terms` 8;
  `fts_candidates` 40; `vector_candidates` 40; `shortlist` 24; `max_items` 8; `max_toks` 3,000;
  `parent_expansion` on with 3 lookups of at most 768 estimated tokens; `rerank_candidates` 12; total
  budget 5 s with 1.5 s for full-text, 1.5 s to embed, 1 s for the vector scan, 2 s to rerank and 1 s to
  rewrite.
- **The term cap stays at 8.** On twelve-term questions it cuts p50 by about 16% against no cap (265 and
  278 ms against 318 and 329 ms at 50,000 chunks, p95 339 and 379 ms against 386 and 456 ms) and a cap of 4
  by about 37%. Three-term questions are untouched. The gain is real but modest and the
  index's own 16-term cap already stops the worst case; a smaller cap trades recall for latency and is a
  profile decision for the 4 GB profile, not a default. The cap keeps identifiers whole and drops stop
  words before content words, so it removes the least informative terms first.
- **The recheck covers the head of the list only** (batches of `max_items`, stopping when the package is
  full), which cut it from 24 chunks to 8 in the common case. It still checks every chunk that enters the
  package.
- **The full-text budget never tripped.** None of about 6,700 timed queries (including four concurrent
  readers on a 50,000-chunk scope) hit the 1.5 s full-text deadline; the worst full-text stage means were
  336 to 362 ms (four readers, as shipped). A tripped budget is a typed `timeout` error, never an empty
  package, and is tested with an index that ignores its context.

Dev box, Windows 11, shared with other jobs and antivirus, **neither emulated nor certified**; every row
is one run unless it says two (the term-cap rounds); the same row repeated differs by 10 to 40% on this
machine, so read ratios and orders of magnitude, not decimals. The first scenario after each build runs
on a cold OS cache (the 10k "cold" cells).

### Findings that need action outside this task
1. **`index.Recheck` costs O(scope) per chunk.** Its statement filters on the three scope columns and
   `generation_id`/`chunk_id`; with no statistics SQLite plans it on `ragindex_chunk_doc (application_id,
   tenant_id, workspace_id)` and scans every row of the scope for every chunk. `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`:
   `SEARCH c USING INDEX ragindex_chunk_doc (application_id=? AND tenant_id=? AND workspace_id=?)`. Eight
   lookups take 16.1 ms at 10,000 chunks against 0.5 ms with `INDEXED BY ragindex_chunk_live
   (generation_id=? AND chunk_id=?)`, 32 times faster. In a query it is the largest stage as shipped:
   240 ms of 423 ms at 50,000 chunks (the stage table above), 1 ms with the fix. This pipeline
   already rechecks as few chunks as it can (8, not 24), but the fix belongs in `internal/rag/index`
   (M2-06); `ParentOf` is on a primary key and is not affected.
2. **The index has no read of chunks by reference**, which a chunk found only by the vector stage needs
   (the vector index returns chunk, generation and source IDs, no text). Until it does, the pipeline's
   `HitReader` seam has no production implementation: such a candidate is dropped and the result is
   labelled `reduced_semantic_recall`, so **in production today "hybrid" reorders the full-text candidates
   and cannot add one**. It should be one statement per ref under the visibility rules of a search, on the
   same index as finding 1; the benchmark's reader is that statement, and the read-back stage costs 1 to
   2 ms at 50,000 chunks with it.
3. **A flat vector scan always returns its top K, however poor the match.** `vector_min_score` (default 0:
   only anti-correlated chunks are dropped) is the floor; its useful value is model-specific and is for
   M2-14 to calibrate.

## 4 GB profile impact
**Preserves the 4 GB target; it makes nothing harder, and every dial it adds is explicit.**

- It adds no process, no service, no model and no resident state, so it does not move the idle floor.
- **FTS-only is the 4 GB shape**: about 30 MiB over nothing at 50,000 chunks with one reader (the bare
  test process is about 15.6 of that), no model. Hybrid with the vector cache off is about 55 MiB and
  keeps the scan at 152 ms a query at 50,000 chunks (the experimental-4g setting of ADR 0011); turning the
  cache on buys 130 ms for 25 to 40 MiB at 50,000 vectors, a profile decision.
- **Four readers cost about 35 to 40 MiB** more than one at 10k and 50k chunks (SQLite page caches); the
  reader count, not this package, is the dial there.
- **The reranker and the rewrite stay off**, which keeps the Reranker and Router models from being loaded
  on this path in Lite (§7.1, §17.2). Their inputs are capped so enabling them cannot grow the context.
- It does not make the target harder: every cap that bounds memory (candidates, shortlist, parents, items,
  tokens, terms) is a validated number with a hard ceiling, and nothing is unbounded.

## Re-evaluation trigger
- **`internal/rag/index` gets a chunk read by reference and a recheck that is not O(scope)** (findings 1
  and 2): re-run `TestBenchQueryPipeline` with the real reader, re-state the 10k and 50k rows without the
  scratch fix, and wire the production `HitReader`.
- **M2-14 / M2-15 deliver the labelled corpus and the live runs**: measure recall of FTS-only against
  hybrid, the reranker's gain over fused order, the rewrite's gain, the real embedder and reranker
  latency and RSS, and calibrate `vector_min_score`, `max_query_terms` and `shortlist`.
- **A Lite workspace passes about 25,000 chunks**, or the 4 GB profile gets a latency target: BM25 inside
  a large scope is where the time is (95 to 99% of a query with the fix); candidates are a smaller term cap, a smaller
  per-workspace corpus ceiling, or a different lexical engine behind `Lexical`.
- **The vector stage moves off the flat scan** (ADR 0011's ceiling) or the embedding model's dimension
  changes: the vector figures here are 384-dimensional int8.
- **A new reranker or router model** is proposed for Lite: it needs its own review before the switch moves.
